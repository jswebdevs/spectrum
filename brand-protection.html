What Is Brand Protection?

Last updated 9 November 2026

A customer buys a medicine, a cosmetic or a spare part with your name on it. It is fake. It does not work, or it causes harm, and the customer blames your brand. Brand protection is the set of tools that make fakes hard to sell and easy to catch. This guide explains how it works in plain words.

Brand protection in simple terms

Brand protection means making sure that products sold under your name really are yours. It deals with three common problems:

Fake products

Copies made to look like your product and sold as the real thing.

Diverted stock

Genuine goods sold outside the market or channel they were meant for.

Refilled or relabelled goods

Old or empty packaging refilled, or expired stock relabelled and sold again.

How it works

Most brand protection systems use the same building blocks:

  • A unique code on every item. Each product, or each box, gets its own serial number and QR code, not one code for the whole batch.
  • Labels that are hard to copy or move. Tamper-evident labels break if someone tries to peel them off and reuse them.
  • A quick check for customers. The buyer scans the code with a phone camera and sees at once whether the product is genuine.
  • Tracking through the supply chain. The code is scanned at the factory, warehouse and distributor, so you know where each item should be.
  • Alerts and reports. If one code is scanned many times or in the wrong place, it is flagged as a likely copy.

Brand protection in Bangladesh

Products that are often faked include medicines, cosmetics, electronics and their accessories, spare parts, and farm inputs such as seeds and fertiliser. For some of these, a fake is not just a lost sale: it can harm health, crops or safety.

Smartphones are now common across the country, so a QR check that works in an ordinary phone browser reaches most buyers without them installing anything.

Where to start

  • Start with the products that are faked most, or where a fake does the most harm.
  • Make the check easy. If checking takes more than a few seconds, customers will not do it.
  • Tell customers to check. A short message on the pack and in your advertising makes a big difference.
  • Use the data. Scans show where your products are sold and where fakes appear, which helps both sales and enforcement.

What it can and cannot do

Benefits

  • Customers can check before they buy or use a product.
  • Copied codes are caught, because the same code cannot be genuine in many places.
  • You see where fakes appear, so you can act on real information.
  • You learn about your market from where and when genuine products are scanned.

Limits to know

  • It does not stop copying by itself. It makes fakes easy to catch, and harder to sell.
  • Labels add a small cost to each item.
  • Customers must be told to check, or the codes go unused.
  • Enforcement is still needed to act on the fakes the system finds.

Frequently asked questions

Can a counterfeiter copy our QR codes?

They can copy a code, but every item has its own code. When one code is scanned many times, or in places it was never shipped to, the system flags it as a copy and the customer sees a warning.

Do customers need to install an app?

No. The check opens in the phone's normal web browser after scanning the QR code. An SMS check can be added for basic phones.

Does it work for small, low-cost products?

Yes. For low-cost items, the code is usually printed on the pack or a small label, which keeps the cost per item low.

Can it connect to our warranty or loyalty programme?

Yes. The same scan can register a warranty or add loyalty points, which gives customers another reason to check.

How long does it take to set up?

It depends on your production line and how codes are printed. We start with one product as a pilot, then add more.
